Actor Tally Guide What's new Privacy Support

Privacy Policy

Effective date: 22 November 2026 · Applies to the Actor Tally browser extension, version 4.0 and later, on every browser and store it is distributed through (Chrome Web Store, Microsoft Edge Add-ons, Firefox Add-ons and Opera Add-ons). This revision adds the few settings introduced in 4.2 to the list of what is stored on your device and names the new stores. Nothing about what leaves your device has changed: still nothing. (Previous effective date: 23 August 2026.)

Actor Tally cannot send your data anywhere, because it contains no code capable of connecting to the internet. Everything you log stays in your browser, on your device. We never see it. Nobody does, unless you show them. The rest of this document explains that in detail.

1. What Actor Tally stores

All of the following is stored locally, on your device only, inside your browser's extension storage:

  • The entries you log: names, timestamps, optional star ratings (in half steps), optional tags, and whether a name was billed as a lead or a co-star
  • The page title and page address of the tab you were on when you logged an entry
  • Optional details you add to a log: how long you watched (typed, or measured by the extension's own stopwatch) and — only if you switch the capture on — how many seconds into the page's video you were at the moment you pressed Log
  • Places you create as labels for your logs: the name, icon and colour you choose, and coordinates only if you type them in or click them on the offline map. The extension has no access to your device's location and never asks for it; a place has no coordinates unless you put them there yourself
  • Photos you attach to a person's card (added from your device, cropped by you, or captured from a page you were viewing)
  • Optional display touches you add to a person's card, such as a decorative frame or a short code name
  • Your settings and preferences: language, theme, banner (including a custom banner image if you add one), which panels are shown, the order you drag them into, row limits, insight rotation, any monthly goal you set, and how many past tags the popup suggests
  • For the backup reminder: whether it is on, the thresholds you chose, and — per page — when you last exported a backup and how many logs it held, so the reminder can tell what is new. The backup file itself is saved wherever you choose; the extension does not keep or read it
  • Which milestones have already been announced, so the same one is not shown twice
  • The text of the quick-hide "escape" page — a plain, editable to-do list you write yourself. It is stored unencrypted by design: it has to render instantly, and it is meant to contain nothing sensitive
  • Your PIN configuration (stored as a cryptographic derivation, never as the PIN itself) and a local log of failed unlock attempts
  • Deleted entries, held in a local trash for 30 days before permanent removal, and up to twenty undo/redo steps held in memory for the current session
  • Badge, milestone, and statistics data derived from your own entries

Actor Tally can hold up to four separate logs ("pages") in one installation. Each keeps its own entries, aliases, places, photos and trash, and each is covered by everything in this policy. If you set a PIN, every page is encrypted with it.

None of this is transmitted, synced, uploaded, mirrored, or backed up anywhere by the extension.

2. What Actor Tally transmits

Nothing. The extension makes no network requests of any kind. It contains no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no advertising code, no update pings, and no remote content. This is not a policy choice that could quietly change with an update — it is the product's core architecture, and the extension's source can be inspected to verify it. On Firefox, the add-on also declares in its manifest that it collects no data. If a future version were ever to add any network capability, that would be a fundamental change to the product and would be stated plainly in the extension's changelog and store listings before you install it.

Updates are delivered by your browser from the store you installed from, under that store's own terms; the extension takes no part in it.

3. What we can see

Nothing. There is no account, no sign-up, no email address, and no server operated by or for Actor Tally. We have no mechanism to view, retrieve, or reconstruct any user's data, including in response to a request — the data simply never reaches us.

4. Encryption

If you set a PIN, your stored data is encrypted at rest on your device using keys derived from your PIN (PBKDF2 with 310,000 iterations and SHA-256, producing an AES-256-GCM key, all via your browser's built-in Web Crypto implementation — no custom cryptography). While your data is unlocked, the derived key is held only in temporary session storage so you don't have to re-enter your PIN for every log; it is discarded when the browser closes. Without a PIN set, data is stored unencrypted in your browser's extension storage, protected by your device's own security. Because encryption keys are derived from your PIN and exist only on your device, a forgotten PIN cannot be recovered by anyone, including us.

While a tab is locked it holds no decrypted data at all — the lock screen is not a cover over a loaded page; the page simply never reads or decrypts anything until the correct PIN is entered. On an unlocked dashboard, pressing Esc replaces it with an ordinary editable to-do list at any moment.

5. Your controls

  • Export: you can export your complete history at any time as a backup file that you own and store wherever you choose. An optional reminder, which you can adjust or switch off, offers an export after a number of new logs or days.
  • Import: you can restore an exported backup in full, including on a different computer or browser.
  • Friend comparison files: you can optionally export a deliberately minimal file to compare with a friend. It contains actor names with per-name counts (and how often each was billed as a co-star), the timestamps of your logs, your device's time-zone offset, and a small block of totals — your number of logs, unique actors, total minutes and how many logs carried a duration, your longest streak and your number of active days. The timestamps and offset are what let the comparison line up "same-time" moments and draw a month-by-month race chart. It deliberately excludes page titles, page addresses, links, tags, ratings, site names, places, photos and durations of individual logs. The comparison runs entirely on whichever device opens the file. Sharing that file, and how you share it, is entirely in your hands; it never passes through us.
  • Imported files: a backup or friend file you import is read on your device only. Its contents are validated before they are accepted — links that are not ordinary web addresses are discarded rather than stored, and every field is clamped to a sane shape — so opening a file someone sent you cannot be used to plant anything active in your history.
  • Delete: deleted entries sit in a local trash for 30 days and can be restored during that window; after 30 days they are permanently removed. You can also empty the trash immediately, remove every star rating on a page at once, or rename and delete tags across a page.
  • Full removal: uninstalling the extension removes its stored data from your browser, per your browser's standard extension-storage behavior. If you want a copy first, export before uninstalling.

6. Permissions, explained

  • storage / unlimitedStorage: used to save your entries, photos, and settings locally on your device. The unlimited-storage permission simply lifts the default size cap so your own photos and history are never truncated; it moves no data off the device.
  • activeTab / scripting: used only when you click the extension, to read the current page's title and visible cast or performer links so it can suggest names for you to log — and, if you have switched that capture on, to read the current position of the page's video at the moment you press Log. The screenshot tool captures the visible tab only when you press it. The extension does not read pages in the background and does not monitor your browsing.

These are the same four permissions the extension has always requested; version 4.2 adds none, and they are identical on every browser. It requests no host permissions, so it has no standing access to any site. Features that might look like they need the network do not: the places map is a compressed public-domain vector dataset (Natural Earth) that ships inside the extension and is drawn on your device, and image exports are generated locally by your browser.

7. Payments and donations

The extension is free and contains no ads and no purchases. If you choose to support development, donations happen entirely on third-party platforms (such as Patreon or a cryptocurrency network) that you visit yourself, outside the extension, under those platforms' own terms and privacy policies. The extension itself neither links out automatically nor knows whether you donated.

8. Children

Actor Tally is not directed at children and is intended for adults.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, the updated version will be published at this address with a new effective date, and any material change will be noted in the extension's changelog. Given the architecture described above, the realistic scope of change is limited: a version of Actor Tally that transmits data would be a different product, and would be presented as such.
